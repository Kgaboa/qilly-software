## 🌟 The Story Behind Qilly

### How a Two-Week Delay Sparked a Construction Revolution

---

## The Problem: October 2023

### Penny's Frustration

**Penny Ndlovu**, a seasoned software engineer from Johannesburg, had just secured her dream project: developing a property management app for a new RDP housing development in Soweto. The project was funded by a government grant, and everything was aligned perfectly—until it wasn't.

"I just need a Bill of Quantities," Penny told her contact at the housing department. "How long will it take?"

"Two weeks, maybe three if the quantity surveyor is busy," came the response.

Penny was shocked. **Two weeks?** For a pricing estimate? In her world of instant APIs, cloud computing, and real-time data, this seemed absurd. But she needed that BOQ to finalize her budget, so she waited.

---

### The Call to Zanele

Frustrated and curious, Penny called her former university classmate, **Zanele Mthembu**, who ran a small civil engineering consultancy in Midrand. Zanele had graduated from the University of Witwatersrand with a degree in Civil Engineering and had spent the last eight years working on government infrastructure projects.

"Zee, I need a Bill of Quantities for a 100-unit housing project. Can you help?"

Zanele laughed. "Of course! Send me the details. It'll take about two weeks."

"**Two weeks?!**" Penny exclaimed. "You're the third person to tell me that. What takes so long?"

There was a pause. Zanele sighed.

"Penny, let me show you. Come to my office tomorrow. I'll walk you through what goes into pricing a BOQ. You'll understand why."

---

## The Discovery: November 2023

### Inside the Chaos

The next day, Penny walked into Zanele's office—a modest space in a Midrand business park. The walls were covered with project plans, compliance certificates, and sticky notes with supplier phone numbers. Zanele's desk was buried under spreadsheets, printed price lists from 2019, and coffee-stained notebooks.

"Welcome to the chaos," Zanele said with a weary smile.

She pulled up an Excel spreadsheet on her computer. "This is your BOQ. 47 line items: excavation, concrete, bricks, steel, plumbing, electrical, the works."

"Looks straightforward," Penny said, scanning the list.

Zanele laughed. "Oh, you sweet summer child. Watch this."

---

### The Manual Process

For the next two hours, Penny watched Zanele work:

**Item 1: Ready-mix concrete 25MPa (200m³)**

Zanele opened her notebook, flipped to a dog-eared page with supplier contacts, and started calling:

- **Lafarge Johannesburg:** "Hi, it's Zanele. I need a quote for 200 cubic meters of 25MPa concrete... Yes, I'll hold."  
  → **Result:** R1,180/m³ + R450 delivery. But they're in Kempton Park—45km away.

- **PPC Midrand:** "Hi, can you quote me on 25MPa... Oh, you're out of stock until next month? Okay, thanks."  
  → **Result:** No quote.

- **AfriSam Pretoria:** "Hi, I need 200m³... R1,320/m³ plus R600 delivery from Pretoria? Let me think about it."  
  → **Result:** Too expensive.

After 30 minutes and five phone calls, Zanele had three quotes. She manually typed them into Excel, calculated transport costs based on Google Maps distances, and selected the cheapest option.

"One item done," Zanele said. "46 to go."

Penny was stunned. "You're telling me you do this **for every single item?**"

"Every. Single. Item."

---

**Item 12: PVC Sewer Pipe 110mm (1,500m)**

Zanele pulled out a printed price list from 2022.

"This is from Marley. I'll use this as a baseline, but I need to call them to confirm it's still accurate."

She dialed. No answer. Voicemail.

"I'll try again later," Zanele said, scribbling a note on a sticky pad. "In the meantime, let me check Builders Warehouse's website."

She Googled "PVC pipe 110mm Builders Warehouse," found a product listing, manually calculated the price per meter, and added it to her spreadsheet.

"But wait," Penny interrupted. "That price doesn't include delivery from the store to the construction site. How do you calculate that?"

Zanele shrugged. "I estimate. I've been doing this long enough to know Builders Warehouse in Sandton is about 20km from Soweto, so I add R300 for transport. It's a rough guess."

"**A rough guess?**" Penny was incredulous. "You're pricing a R3 million project with rough guesses?"

Zanele looked at her, exhausted. "Penny, this is how the entire industry works. There's no system. No database. No automation. It's me, my phone, my outdated price lists, and prayer."

---

### The Compliance Nightmare

"What about compliance?" Penny asked. "Don't you have to check if these materials meet SANS 1200 standards?"

Zanele groaned. "Oh, sweet mercy. Don't even get me started."

She opened a 400-page PDF document—the SANS 1200 standards manual.

"For every item, I have to cross-reference this manual to make sure the product meets the spec. Then I have to check if the supplier has a valid AGRÉMENT certificate. Then I have to verify their BBBEE level for the government's procurement scorecard."

"How long does that take?"

"Hours. Sometimes days. And if I miss something, the project gets delayed by **six weeks** while we source compliant materials. I've seen it happen."

Penny watched as Zanele toggled between the SANS PDF, the AGRÉMENT website (which kept timing out), and a supplier's BBBEE certificate (which was expired).

"This is insane," Penny said.

"Welcome to South African construction procurement," Zanele replied. "Population: chaos."

---

## The Breaking Point: The Fraud Story

As the afternoon wore on, Zanele shared a story that made Penny's blood boil.

"Six months ago, I worked on a municipal infrastructure project in Limpopo. The municipality asked for quotes from three quantity surveyors. We all submitted BOQs for the same project."

"And?" Penny leaned in.

"My quote: **R12.3 million**. I called suppliers, checked prices, optimized transport costs—the whole thing.

The other two quotes: **R15.8 million** and **R17.2 million**."

"Why the huge difference?"

Zanele's voice dropped. "Because they were inflating prices. They had 'arrangements' with certain suppliers—kickbacks. The supplier would quote R2,000 per ton for cement when the market rate was R1,400. The QS would get a 15% cut under the table."

Penny's eyes widened. "That's fraud!"

"Welcome to the industry. The municipality went with the R17.2 million quote because the official had a 'relationship' with that QS. My honest quote? Rejected for being 'unrealistically low.'"

Zanele paused. "Penny, I estimate **R14.8 billion** is lost to procurement fraud in South African construction every year. And nobody can stop it because there's no transparency. It's all phone calls, handshake deals, and opaque pricing."

Penny was silent for a long moment.

Then she said: "What if there was a system?"

---

## The Eureka Moment: November 2023

### "What If We Could Automate This?"

That evening, Penny and Zanele sat in a Mugg & Bean in Midrand, laptops open, ideas flowing.

"Here's what I'm thinking," Penny said, sketching on a napkin. "What if we built a platform that does everything you just spent two days doing—in **five minutes**?"

Zanele laughed. "Penny, that's impossible."

"Is it? Think about it:"

**Penny's Vision:**
```
Step 1: Upload your BOQ Excel file (5 seconds)
Step 2: System automatically matches items to a database of suppliers (AI matching)
Step 3: System queries supplier prices in real-time (API integration)
Step 4: System calculates transport costs based on GPS coordinates (Google Maps API)
Step 5: System checks SANS 1200, AGRÉMENT, BBBEE compliance (automated lookups)
Step 6: System recommends the best supplier based on price + transport + compliance
Step 7: Generate audit-ready report (PDF export)

Total time: 5 minutes.
```

Zanele stared at the napkin. "If this existed, it would change everything."

"So let's build it," Penny said.

"You're serious?"

"Dead serious. Zee, you just told me the industry wastes **R5.7 billion** on professional fees and **R14.8 billion** on fraud. That's **R20.5 billion** annually. If we can build a platform that eliminates even **10%** of that waste, we're talking about a **R2 billion** market opportunity."

Zanele's engineering mind kicked in. "Okay, but we'd need:"

1. **A supplier database** - Prices from Buco, Builders Warehouse, Cashbuild, Lafarge, PPC, Marley, everyone.
2. **Real-time pricing** - API connections or weekly data syncs.
3. **Compliance databases** - SANS 1200, NBR, AGRÉMENT, CIDB, BBBEE.
4. **Matching algorithm** - AI to match "Ready-mix concrete 25MPa" to "Concrete 25MPa Lafarge."
5. **Transport optimization** - Distance calculation and cost modeling.
6. **Security & POPIA compliance** - Government won't use it otherwise.

Penny nodded. "I can build that. I've worked on AI matching algorithms for e-commerce. This is the same concept—match user queries to product catalogs, rank by relevance, optimize by price and location."

"But suppliers won't just give us their data," Zanele countered. "Why would Lafarge share their pricing with a startup?"

Penny smiled. "Because we send them **customers**. If we process 10,000 BOQs per year and Lafarge wins 20% of concrete quotes, that's **R500 million** in revenue for them. They'll happily give us data in exchange for deal flow."

Zanele's eyes lit up. "Quid pro quo. We're the marketplace."

"Exactly."

---

### The Name: "Qilly"

Two hours later, fueled by coffee and adrenaline, they needed a name.

"It needs to be catchy, memorable, and related to quantity surveying," Zanele said.

"How about... **Qilly**?" Penny suggested. "Short for 'Quantity' and 'Easily.' Like 'Quickly' but for Bills of Quantities."

Zanele tested it. "Qilly. Qilly. I like it. It sounds friendly, fast, efficient."

"Plus, the domain qilly.co.za is available," Penny added, checking her phone.

"Done. We're Qilly."

They shook hands.

## The Journey: December 2023 - February 2026

### Building Qilly (15 Months of Hustle)

#### **December 2023 - February 2024: The MVP**

Penny quit her job. Zanele reduced her consulting hours to part-time. They pooled their savings—**R500,000**—and got to work.

**Penny's Focus: Technology**
- Built the core platform on React + Vite (fast, modern web app)
- Integrated Supabase for database (secure, scalable)
- Developed AI matching algorithm (fuzzy text matching, keyword extraction, confidence scoring)
- Created Excel parser (auto-detect BOQ structure)
- Built transport cost calculator (Google Maps Distance Matrix API)

**Zanele's Focus: Industry Knowledge**
- Mapped SANS 1200 standards to common BOQ items
- Built compliance database (AGRÉMENT certificates, NBR regulations)
- Created product taxonomy (categories: earthworks, concrete, masonry, steel, etc.)
- Negotiated with 6 initial suppliers (Buco, Builders Warehouse, Cashbuild, Lafarge, PPC, Marley)
- Validated pricing logic with industry experts

By February 2024, they had a **working prototype**.

---
